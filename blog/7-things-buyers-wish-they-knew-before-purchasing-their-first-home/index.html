7 Things Buyers Wish They Knew Before Purchasing Their First Home

Buying your first home is one of the most exciting milestones you'll ever experience. It's also one of the biggest financial decisions you'll make.

Many first time buyers tell us the same thing after they get their keys:

"I wish I had known that sooner."

The good news? You don't have to learn everything the hard way.

Here are seven things many buyers wish they knew before starting the home buying process.

1. You Don't Need a 20% Down Payment

One of the biggest myths about buying a home is that you need to save 20% before you can even think about purchasing.

While putting 20% down can help you avoid private mortgage insurance (PMI), many loan programs allow qualified buyers to purchase a home with much less.

Depending on the loan, you may qualify with a lower down payment than you expected.

The best first step is talking with a trusted lender to understand your options.

2. Your Monthly Payment Includes More Than Your Mortgage

Many buyers focus only on the home's purchase price.

Your monthly payment may also include:

  • Property taxes
  • Homeowners insurance
  • Mortgage insurance (if applicable)
  • HOA dues (for some communities)

Understanding your complete monthly payment helps you shop confidently and avoid surprises.

3. Getting Pre Approved Gives You an Advantage

Many buyers start by looking at homes online.

A better first step is getting pre approved.

A pre approval helps you:

  • Understand your budget
  • Strengthen your offer
  • Show sellers you're a serious buyer
  • Move quickly when you find the right home

In today's market, being prepared can make a big difference.

4. There Are More Costs Than Just the Down Payment

Beyond your down payment, there are additional expenses to plan for.

These may include:

  • Closing costs
  • Home inspection
  • Appraisal
  • Moving expenses
  • Utility deposits
  • Initial maintenance and repairs

Planning ahead makes the transition into homeownership much smoother.

5. A Home Inspection Is One of the Best Investments You'll Make

Even beautiful homes can have hidden issues.

A professional home inspection helps identify potential concerns before closing and gives you a better understanding of your future home.

It's one of the most valuable steps in protecting your investment.

6. Don't Make Big Financial Changes Before Closing

Once you're under contract, it's important to keep your finances as stable as possible.

Avoid:

  • Opening new credit cards
  • Financing a new vehicle
  • Making large purchases
  • Changing jobs without speaking with your lender

Even small financial changes can affect your loan approval.

7. You Don't Have to Figure It Out Alone

Buying your first home can feel overwhelming, but you don't have to navigate it by yourself.

An experienced real estate professional will guide you through every step, answer your questions, negotiate on your behalf, and help you avoid common mistakes.

Our goal is to make the process as smooth and stress free as possible so you can focus on the excitement of becoming a homeowner.
