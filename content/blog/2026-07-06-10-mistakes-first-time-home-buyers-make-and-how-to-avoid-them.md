The good news? Most of these mistakes are completely avoidable.

Here are the 10 most common mistakes first time home buyers make and how you can avoid them.

### 1. Starting Your Home Search Before Getting Pre Approved

It's tempting to start browsing homes online right away, but falling in love with a home before knowing your budget can lead to disappointment.

Getting pre approved first helps you:

* Understand how much home you can comfortably afford.
* Strengthen your offer when you find the right home.
* Move quickly in a competitive market.

Think of your pre approval as your roadmap before you begin house hunting.

### 2. Thinking You Need a 20% Down Payment

One of the biggest myths in real estate is that every buyer needs to put 20% down.

Many loan programs allow qualified buyers to purchase a home with much less. Depending on your situation, you may qualify for a loan that requires a significantly lower down payment.

Speaking with a trusted lender can help you understand the options available to you.

### 3. Forgetting About Closing Costs

Many buyers budget for the down payment but forget about additional expenses.

Closing costs may include:

* Loan fees
* Appraisal fees
* Title and escrow fees
* Homeowners insurance
* Prepaid property taxes

Planning for these costs ahead of time helps prevent surprises.

### 4. Ignoring Your Credit Before Applying

Your credit score plays an important role in your mortgage approval and interest rate.

Before applying for a loan:

* Pay bills on time.
* Avoid late payments.
* Keep credit card balances low.
* Review your credit report for errors.

Even small improvements may help you qualify for better financing.

### 5. Making Large Purchases Before Closing

After getting pre approved, it's important to avoid making major financial changes.

Try not to:

* Buy a new car.
* Finance furniture.
* Open new credit cards.
* Make large unexplained bank deposits.

Your lender will continue reviewing your finances until closing day.

### 6. Skipping the Home Inspection

A home may look perfect during a showing, but every property has details that deserve a closer look.

A professional inspection can uncover:

* Roof concerns
* Plumbing issues
* Electrical problems
* HVAC repairs
* Foundation concerns

A home inspection gives you valuable information before making one of the biggest purchases of your life.

### 7. Focusing Only on the Purchase Price

The monthly mortgage payment is only part of the cost of homeownership.

Also consider:

* Property taxes
* Homeowners insurance
* HOA dues (if applicable)
* Utilities
* Maintenance and repairs

Understanding the full cost helps you buy with confidence.

### 8. Letting Emotions Take Over

It's easy to become emotionally attached to a home.

While it's important to love where you live, it's equally important to stay focused on your budget, priorities, and long term goals.

Sometimes the right decision isn't the first house you fall in love with.

### 9. Not Working With an Experienced Realtor

Buying a home involves contracts, negotiations, inspections, timelines, disclosures, and dozens of important decisions.

An experienced Realtor helps you:

* Find the right home.
* Negotiate favorable terms.
* Explain every step of the process.
* Protect your best interests.
* Keep your transaction on track.

Having the right professional on your side can make all the difference.

### 10. Waiting Too Long to Ask Questions

No question is too small when you're buying your first home.

The more informed you are, the more confident you'll feel throughout the process.

Whether you're wondering about financing, inspections, neighborhoods, or timelines, asking questions early can help prevent costly mistakes later.
