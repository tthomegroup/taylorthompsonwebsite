If you're buying or selling a home for the first time, you've probably heard the word "escrow." But what exactly does it mean?




Let's walk through what happens during escrow so you know what to expect.

### Step 1. Your Offer Is Accepted

Congratulations! Once the seller accepts your offer, you're officially under contract.

The purchase agreement outlines important details, including:

* Purchase price
* Contingencies
* Closing date
* Earnest money deposit
* Items included in the sale

This is when the escrow process officially begins.

### Step 2. Your Earnest Money Deposit Is Submitted

Shortly after your offer is accepted, you'll submit your earnest money deposit.

This deposit shows the seller you're serious about purchasing the home. The funds are safely held by the escrow company until closing and are typically applied toward your down payment or closing costs.

### Step 3. Home Inspections Are Completed

Next comes one of the most important steps in the process: the home inspection.

A licensed home inspector will evaluate the property's condition and identify any potential concerns, such as:

* Roof issues
* Plumbing concerns
* Electrical systems
* HVAC performance
* Foundation issues
* Safety concerns

Depending on the results, buyers may negotiate repairs, request credits, or move forward with the purchase as agreed.

### Step 4. The Appraisal Is Ordered

If you're financing the home, your lender will order an appraisal.

The appraiser's job is to determine the home's market value to ensure it supports the purchase price.

If the appraisal comes in at value, the transaction continues as planned.

If it comes in lower than expected, your Realtor will help guide you through the available options.

### Step 5. Your Loan Moves Through Final Approval

During escrow, your lender works behind the scenes to finalize your mortgage.

They may request:

* Updated bank statements
* Pay stubs
* Employment verification
* Tax documents
* Additional financial information

One important tip:

Avoid making major financial changes during this time. Purchasing a vehicle, opening new credit cards, or changing jobs could impact your loan approval.

### Step 6. Title Is Reviewed

The title company researches the property's ownership history to confirm there are no outstanding issues that could affect the sale.

They'll look for things like:

* Liens
* Ownership disputes
* Unpaid taxes
* Easements

Title insurance is then issued to help protect both the buyer and lender.

### Step 7. Contingencies Are Removed

As inspections, financing, and other contract requirements are completed, contingencies are removed according to the purchase agreement.

Once all contingencies have been satisfied, the transaction moves one step closer to closing.

### Step 8. Review Your Closing Disclosure

A few days before closing, your lender will provide a Closing Disclosure.

This document outlines:

* Your loan terms
* Interest rate
* Monthly payment
* Closing costs
* Cash needed to close

Review everything carefully and ask questions if anything is unclear.

### Step 9. Final Walk Through

Shortly before closing, buyers complete a final walk through of the home.

This allows you to confirm:

* Agreed upon repairs have been completed.
* The home is in the expected condition.
* Included appliances remain with the property.
* Nothing has changed since your last visit.

### Step 10. Closing Day!

The big day has arrived.

You'll sign the final paperwork, your lender will fund the loan, and once the transaction is officially recorded with the county, ownership transfers to you.

Congratulations, you're officially a homeowner!
