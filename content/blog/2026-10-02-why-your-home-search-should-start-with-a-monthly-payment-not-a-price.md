## Start With the Payment That Feels Comfortable

When you start looking for a home, it’s easy to pick a price range first. Maybe you tell yourself, “I want to stay under $500,000,” and start scrolling through listings in that range.

But here’s the thing: *two homes with the exact same price can have very different monthly costs.*

Your interest rate, down payment, property taxes, homeowners insurance, HOA fees, and loan type can all affect what you actually pay each month. That’s why I encourage buyers to think about their monthly budget before getting too attached to a specific purchase price.

## Your Purchase Price Isn't Your Monthly Payment

A $500,000 home doesn't automatically mean the same monthly payment for every buyer.

For example, two buyers could purchase homes at the same price but have different down payments, interest rates, insurance costs, or property tax amounts. Their monthly payments could look very different.

That's why your home search should start with a conversation about what you're *comfortable paying each month*, not simply the maximum price a lender says you can afford.

## "Approved For" Doesn't Always Mean "Comfortable With"

This is an important distinction.

A lender may approve you for a certain loan amount based on your income, debts, credit profile, and other financial factors. But your approved amount isn't necessarily the amount you *want* to spend every month.

Think about your actual lifestyle, too. Maybe you want room in your budget for traveling, saving, eating out, home projects, kids' activities, or simply having a little breathing room.

The goal isn't necessarily to spend every dollar you're approved for. It's to find a payment that makes sense for *your life.*

## What Goes Into Your Monthly Housing Cost?

When you're comparing homes, look beyond the listing price.

Your monthly housing expenses may include:

* Principal and interest  
* Property taxes  
* Homeowners insurance  
* Mortgage insurance, depending on your loan  
* HOA dues, if applicable  
* Other property specific costs

Some of these expenses can vary significantly from one property to another, which is another reason not to judge affordability based on purchase price alone.

## A Home Search Should Work Backward

Instead of starting with:

**"How much house can I buy?"**

Try starting with:

**"What monthly payment feels comfortable for me?"**

From there, you and your lender can work backward to understand what purchase price range may fit your situation.

This can make your search much more focused. Instead of looking at every home under a certain price, you can concentrate on properties that have a monthly cost that aligns with your overall budget.

## Don't Forget About the Rest of Your Budget

Buying a home doesn't mean your other financial goals disappear.

You'll still have utilities, groceries, transportation, subscriptions, savings, repairs, maintenance, and all the other everyday expenses that come with life.

And yes, something will eventually need fixing. It's a house. 😂

Leaving room in your budget for the unexpected can make homeownership feel much more manageable.

## What If You Find a Home You Love?

This is where having a clear monthly budget can really help.

If you know the payment range you're comfortable with, you can look at a home and have a more productive conversation about whether the property actually fits your finances.

You may also discover that a home slightly above or below your original price target affects your monthly payment differently than you expected.

The numbers matter, but so does the bigger picture.

## Your Home Search Should Fit Your Life

Buying a home isn't just about finding the biggest house you can qualify for. It's about finding a home that works with the life you're living now and the life you're planning for next.

So before you start saving listings and scheduling showings, talk with your lender about your monthly comfort zone. Then we can use that information to build a search around homes that make sense for *you.*

Because the goal isn't just to buy a house.

**It's to buy a home you can actually enjoy living in.** 🖤🏡
