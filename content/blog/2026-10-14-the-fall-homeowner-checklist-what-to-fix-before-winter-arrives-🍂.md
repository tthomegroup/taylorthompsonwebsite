Fall is one of those seasons when home maintenance can easily get pushed to the bottom of the to do list. Between school activities, holidays, and enjoying the cooler weather, it's easy to forget about the little things around the house that need attention.

But if you own a home in Modesto, Turlock, Ripon, Manteca, or anywhere in the Central Valley, October is a great time to prepare for the months ahead. After a long, hot summer, your roof, outdoor spaces, and cooling systems may need some attention before the weather changes.

The good news? You don't need to tackle everything in one weekend. Start with these seven tasks, and your home will be in a much better position for the cooler, wetter months ahead.

### 1. Clean Your Gutters and Check Your Downspouts

Let's start with one of the least exciting home maintenance tasks, but one of the most important.

Leaves, twigs, and other debris can collect in your gutters and prevent rainwater from draining properly. When water backs up or spills over the sides, it can contribute to moisture problems around your home's exterior and foundation.

Take time to remove debris, check that downspouts are clear, and make sure water drains away from the house instead of pooling near the foundation. If you have tall trees or a multistory home, consider hiring a professional rather than climbing a ladder yourself.

Your fall checklist:

* Clear leaves and debris from gutters.
* Check downspouts for blockages.
* Look for sagging gutters or loose connections.
* Confirm that water drains away from your home's foundation.

### 2. Inspect Your Roof Before the Rain Arrives

Your roof spent months dealing with Central Valley sunshine and summer heat. Before the rainy season begins, it's worth checking for signs of damage that could lead to leaks.

Look for missing, cracked, or damaged roofing materials that are visible from the ground. Inside your home, check ceilings and upper walls for water stains or signs of previous leaks. Pay attention to areas around skylights, vents, and chimneys, where flashing may need attention.

You don't need to climb onto the roof to do a basic visual check. If you notice anything concerning, have a qualified roofing professional inspect it before the next major storm.

Your fall checklist:

* Look for visible roof damage.
* Check ceilings for water stains.
* Inspect accessible attic areas for signs of moisture.
* Arrange professional repairs for damaged roofing or flashing.

Catching a problem early can help prevent a minor repair from becoming a much bigger headache during the rainy season.

### 3. Service Your Heating System and Replace the Air Filter

Remember how hard your air conditioner worked during those hot Central Valley summers? Now it's time to make sure your heating system is ready for cooler nights.

If you have a furnace or heat pump, schedule maintenance according to the manufacturer's recommendations. A professional can check the system's operation and identify issues before you need reliable heat on a cold morning.

Don't forget the air filter, either. Check it and replace it when needed based on the manufacturer's instructions and your home's conditions. A clean filter helps maintain airflow and can help your system operate properly.

Your fall checklist:

* Replace or clean the HVAC filter as directed.
* Test your heating system before temperatures drop.
* Schedule professional maintenance if needed.
* Keep supply and return vents clear of furniture and debris.

And while you're at it, test your smoke alarms and carbon monoxide alarms. These safety checks are especially important before heaters and fireplaces see more use.

### 4. Seal Drafts Around Windows and Doors

If certain rooms always feel colder than the rest of the house, air leaks could be part of the problem.

Check the weatherstripping around exterior doors and inspect the caulking around windows for gaps, cracks, or deterioration. Sealing these openings can reduce drafts and help your home stay more comfortable when temperatures drop.

This is also a relatively simple project for homeowners who enjoy a little weekend DIY. Just make sure you use the right materials for the surface and location, and avoid sealing areas that are designed for ventilation or drainage.

Your fall checklist:

* Inspect door weatherstripping.
* Check window caulking for cracks or gaps.
* Replace worn seals where needed.
* Check that exterior doors close securely.

You may be surprised by how much more comfortable your home feels after addressing a few small gaps.

### 5. Prepare Your Yard and Irrigation System

After months of keeping your lawn and landscaping alive through the heat, your outdoor spaces may need a little attention before winter.

Start by trimming dead branches, clearing fallen leaves, and removing debris from walkways and drainage areas. Look for spots where water tends to collect and make sure your downspouts and yard drainage are directing water away from the house.

If you have an automatic irrigation system, check for leaks, broken sprinkler heads, and unnecessary watering. As the weather cools and seasonal rainfall returns, your watering schedule may need adjusting. Follow local watering rules and your landscape's actual needs.

Your fall checklist:

* Trim dead or damaged branches.
* Clear leaves from walkways and drains.
* Check sprinkler heads and irrigation lines.
* Look for areas where water pools near the home.
* Store or protect outdoor items that could be damaged by winter weather.

A little yard cleanup can improve curb appeal while helping prepare your property for wet and windy days.

### 6. Test Safety Devices and Prepare for Power Outages

Home maintenance isn't just about protecting the property. It's also about making sure everyone inside stays safe.

Test your smoke alarms and carbon monoxide alarms, and replace batteries or devices as needed according to the manufacturer's instructions. If you use a fireplace, arrange an appropriate inspection and cleaning before regular use.

It's also smart to prepare for the possibility of a power outage during a winter storm. Keep flashlights, spare batteries, phone chargers, basic first aid supplies, and essential household items in an easy to reach location.

Your fall checklist:

* Test smoke alarms and carbon monoxide alarms.
* Inspect your fireplace and chimney before use.
* Keep flashlights and backup batteries accessible.
* Review your family's emergency plan.
* Know where your home's main water and utility shutoffs are located.

One important safety reminder: never use a gas oven or stove to heat your home, and operate portable generators outdoors and away from the house according to official safety guidance.

### 7. Check Your Exterior Paint, Caulking, and Walkways

Small exterior maintenance issues can be easy to overlook until rain and moisture make them more noticeable.

Walk around your property and look for peeling paint, cracked caulking, loose trim, damaged fence boards, and cracks in walkways. Pay attention to areas where water may enter the home or where loose materials could become a safety concern.

Not every cosmetic issue needs an immediate repair, but damage that allows moisture inside or creates a tripping hazard deserves attention before winter.

Your fall checklist:

* Inspect exterior paint and trim.
* Repair deteriorating caulking around openings.
* Check steps, railings, and walkways for damage.
* Look for signs of moisture around the home's exterior.
* Schedule repairs for issues that could worsen with rain.

If you're unsure whether a crack or moisture issue is cosmetic or a sign of a larger problem, have a qualified professional evaluate it before making repairs.

### Your Fall Home Maintenance Checklist at a Glance

Not sure where to start? Save this list and work through it over the next few weekends.

* Clean gutters and downspouts.
* Inspect the roof for visible damage.
* Replace the HVAC filter and check the heating system.
* Seal drafts around windows and doors.
* Clean up the yard and inspect irrigation.
* Test smoke alarms and carbon monoxide alarms.
* Check exterior paint, caulking, steps, and walkways.

You can also take a few photos of your home's condition before winter. If you discover a leak or other damage later, those photos may help you document what changed.

### A Little Maintenance Now Can Save You Stress Later

Owning a home comes with plenty of responsibilities, but seasonal maintenance doesn't have to feel overwhelming. The key is to catch small issues early, prioritize anything that could lead to water damage or safety concerns, and handle the simple tasks before the weather turns.

For homeowners throughout Modesto, Turlock, Ripon, Riverbank, Manteca, Salida, and the surrounding Central Valley, fall is a great reminder to give your home a little extra attention before settling in for the cooler months.
