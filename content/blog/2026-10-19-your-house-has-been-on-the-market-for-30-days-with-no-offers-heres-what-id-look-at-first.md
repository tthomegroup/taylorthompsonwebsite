Your house has been on the market for 30 days, and you still haven't received an offer. Now what?

If your home isn't selling, the answer isn't always to lower the price. But the market is telling us something, and our job is to figure out exactly what that is.

Selling a home can be an emotional process, especially when you've prepared your property, kept everything clean for showings, and waited for the right buyer to come along. When the offers aren't coming in, it's natural to start questioning everything.

This is where having the right strategy matters. Today's market requires thoughtful guidance, realistic expectations, and patience. Rather than making changes based on frustration, I'd start by looking at the data, the feedback, and what buyers are choosing right now.

Here are the first things I'd review.

## 1. What Is the Market Telling Us?

First, I'd look at what's actually happening in your local market.

Not what we hope the house is worth. Not just what an online estimate says. And not what your neighbor's house sold for two years ago.

I'd want to know what comparable homes are selling for right now, how long they're taking to sell, which properties are receiving offers, and how your home compares with the competition.

Your home's value isn't determined by what someone else paid for a similar property in a different market. Buyers are making decisions based on the options available to them today.

**The real question is this: What are buyers choosing instead of your house?**

That's the competition we need to understand before deciding what to do next.

## 2. Are Buyers Coming Through the Door?

The number of showings your home receives can tell us a lot about what's happening.

If you're getting plenty of showings but no offers, we may need to take a closer look at the home's condition, presentation, perceived value, or how it compares with other properties buyers are considering.

But if showings are few and far between, I'd look more closely at the price, online presentation, marketing exposure, and how the home is positioned against similar listings.

These are two different problems, and they may require different solutions.

**Lots of showings with no offers?** Let's examine the buyer experience and feedback.

**Very few showings?** Let's take a closer look at pricing, marketing, and positioning.

The goal isn't to make random changes. It's to identify where potential buyers are losing interest and figure out what we can improve.

## 3. What Does the Feedback Keep Saying?

One buyer's opinion doesn't necessarily tell us everything we need to know. But when multiple buyers start pointing out the same concern, I'm paying attention.

Maybe several people mention that the home feels overpriced compared with other properties they've toured. Perhaps they consistently comment on the condition, layout, or updates needed. Or maybe the feedback suggests that another property offers more of what buyers want at a similar price.

Patterns matter.

I'd review showing feedback alongside current market data to see whether there's a consistent issue we need to address.

The market won't always give us a perfectly clear answer, but repeated feedback can help us understand what might be keeping buyers from moving forward.

## 4. How Do We Compare With the Homes That Are Selling?

Your competition isn't just the house down the street.

It's every property a qualified buyer could choose instead of yours.

That includes homes that are currently listed, properties that recently sold, and other options that appeal to the same buyers in your price range.

I'd compare factors such as condition, location, layout, upgrades, outdoor space, and overall presentation. I'd also look at whether competing listings have adjusted their prices or are offering features that make them more appealing.

Sometimes a home isn't necessarily a bad listing. It may simply be positioned in a way that makes another property look like the better value.

**The goal is to understand how your home stands out, where it falls short, and what adjustments could make it more competitive.**

## 5. Would I Immediately Reduce the Price?

Not automatically.

Before recommending a price adjustment, I'd review the entire listing strategy. That includes the marketing, photography, online presentation, showing activity, buyer feedback, competing properties, and recent comparable sales.

If the home is priced appropriately but the photos don't showcase it well, we may need to improve the presentation. If buyers aren't seeing the listing, we may need to evaluate the marketing and exposure. If the property consistently receives feedback that it's too expensive compared with similar homes, a pricing adjustment may be worth discussing.

Every situation is different, and the right decision should be based on the evidence rather than a one size fits all approach.

At the same time, I wouldn't let a listing sit indefinitely simply because we're afraid to make an adjustment.

The longer a home stays on the market without meaningful activity, the more likely some buyers are to wonder whether there's a reason others haven't made an offer. That doesn't mean a home is automatically undesirable, but it does mean we should regularly evaluate the strategy.

Sometimes repositioning a listing sooner is more effective than waiting for the market to change.

## 6. Do We Need to Adjust the Strategy?

Selling a home isn't just about putting it online and hoping the right buyer finds it.

It takes a combination of thoughtful pricing, strong presentation, effective marketing, and ongoing communication.

If your home isn't getting the attention you expected, I'd want to know whether we're reaching the right audience and presenting the property in the best possible way.

That could mean reviewing the listing photos, improving the description, addressing easy presentation fixes, evaluating showing availability, or revisiting the pricing strategy.

The important thing is to understand why a change is being recommended and what we hope it will accomplish.

You deserve more than a vague suggestion to wait a little longer or lower the price. You deserve a clear explanation of your options and a strategy that reflects what's happening in your market.

## 7. Who You Hire Matters

This is where having the right real estate agent makes a difference.

My job isn't to tell a seller what they want to hear. It's to explain what I'm seeing, share the available options, and help them make informed decisions about their property and their equity.

That means having honest conversations, reviewing the numbers, paying attention to buyer behavior, and being willing to adjust when the evidence supports a change.

It also means understanding that selling a home can take patience. Not every listing will receive an offer in the first week, and a lack of immediate activity doesn't automatically mean something is wrong with the property.

But patience should come with a plan. We should always know what we're monitoring, what we're learning, and what our next steps might be.

The goal is to make decisions with a clear understanding of the market, not simply react to the number of days your home has been listed.
