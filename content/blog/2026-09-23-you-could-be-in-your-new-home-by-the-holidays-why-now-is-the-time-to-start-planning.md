### What Could the Timeline Look Like?

Every home purchase is different, and there are plenty of variables that can affect the timeline. But once you understand the general process, buying a home can feel much less overwhelming.

If you're ready to start, your first steps could look something like this:

### Get Preapproved

Before you start scrolling through listings at midnight and mentally moving your furniture into every house you see, talk to a lender.

A preapproval can help you understand your potential buying power and what monthly payment may fit comfortably within your budget.

It also allows you to shop with a much clearer idea of what you're looking for.

### Start Your Home Search

Once you understand your financing, you can start looking at homes that fit your budget and your lifestyle.

This is where working with a local Realtor can be especially helpful.

Instead of simply sending you listings, your Realtor can help you understand neighborhoods, pricing, comparable sales, property conditions, and what to look for when you're touring homes.

And yes, you can absolutely start with a wish list.

Just don't be surprised if your priorities change after you start seeing homes in person. 😂

### Find the One

Eventually, you may walk into a house and think:

“This could actually be home.”

When that happens, your Realtor can help you evaluate the property and determine whether it makes sense to write an offer.

Your offer strategy will depend on the property, the market, your financing, and your goals.

Once your offer is accepted, you're officially moving into the next phase of the process.

### Enter Escrow

Escrow is the period between having an accepted offer and completing the purchase.

During this time, there are several important steps that may take place, including inspections, appraisal, loan processing, document review, and other requirements related to the transaction.

This is also when you'll be working closely with your lender, Realtor, escrow team, and other professionals involved in the transaction.

It can feel like a lot.

That's why having a team guiding you through each step matters.

### Get Your Keys

Once everything is completed and the transaction closes, you get the part you've been waiting for.

The keys. 🔑🏡

Depending on when you close and the specific terms of your transaction, you could potentially be settling into your new home before the holidays.

Imagine unpacking your Christmas decorations in your own home.

Hanging your stockings.

Cooking Thanksgiving dinner in your own kitchen.

Waking up Christmas morning knowing that this is YOUR house.

That's the fun part.

### But What About the First Mortgage Payment?

This is one detail many first time buyers don't realize.

Your first mortgage payment is generally not due immediately after closing. The exact timing depends on your closing date, loan terms, and lender.

For example, depending on when you close, you may not make your first mortgage payment until the following month or even later.

So if you're hoping to purchase before the holidays, your lender can explain exactly when your first payment would be due based on your specific loan.

That makes it especially important to look at the entire financial picture rather than simply focusing on the purchase price.

### Don't Wait Until You Find the Perfect House to Start

Here's one of the biggest mistakes first time buyers can make.

Waiting until they find the house they love before figuring out how they're going to buy it.

By then, you're already emotionally invested.

You don't want to be scrambling to get preapproved, figure out your budget, or understand your financing options while you're trying to compete for a home.

Starting early gives you time to prepare.

You can understand your numbers.

You can improve your financial position if necessary.

You can explore different loan options with your lender.

You can figure out what monthly payment feels comfortable.

And when the right home comes along, you're ready.

What If You're Not Sure You're Ready?

That's okay.

You don't have to be ready to buy a house tomorrow to have a conversation today.

Maybe you're wondering whether you can qualify.

Maybe you're unsure how much you need for a down payment.

Maybe you're not sure what your monthly payment would look like.

Maybe you have a home to sell first.

Maybe you simply have no idea where to start.

Those are all good reasons to start the conversation.

You can learn what your options are before making any decisions.
