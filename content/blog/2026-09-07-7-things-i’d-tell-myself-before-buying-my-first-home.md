Buying your first home is exciting.

It’s also a little terrifying.

There are numbers everywhere. Mortgage rates. Down payments. Closing costs. Property taxes. Insurance. Inspections. And then someone casually mentions that you are now responsible for the water heater if it decides to quit on a random Tuesday.

Welcome to homeownership. 😂🏡

Looking back, there are several things I wish I had understood before buying my first home. Not because I regret becoming a homeowner, but because knowing these things sooner would have made the process feel a whole lot less overwhelming.

Here are the biggest lessons I’d tell myself before buying my first home.



**1. You Do NOT Need 20% Down**

This is one of the biggest misconceptions I wish more first time home buyers knew.

You do not automatically need 20% down to buy a home.

There are loan programs that can allow qualified buyers to purchase with significantly less money down.

Of course, putting more money down can have advantages depending on your situation, and every buyer's financial picture is different. But waiting until you have exactly 20% saved isn't necessarily the only path to homeownership.

If buying a home makes sense for your finances and your long term plans, talk with a qualified lender about the loan options available to you.

Because sometimes waiting years just to hit a specific down payment percentage could mean missing years of potential homeownership and equity building.



**2. Your First Home Does Not Need to Be Your Dream Home**

I think this one gets overlooked ALL the time.

Your first home doesn't have to check every single box.

It doesn't need the massive kitchen.

It doesn't need the perfect backyard.

It doesn't need every upgrade you've ever saved on Pinterest.

Your first home can simply be the home that works for your life right now.

Maybe it's a little smaller than you'd ideally like.

Maybe the kitchen isn't your favorite.

Maybe you know you'll eventually want to make some improvements.

That's okay.

Your first home can be a stepping stone.

As you build equity and your financial situation changes, you may eventually be in a better position to move into the home you really want.

The goal isn't perfection.

The goal is making a smart decision that works for YOU.



**3. Your Monthly Payment Matters More Than the Biggest Number a Lender Says You Qualify For Just because a lender says you qualify for a certain purchase price doesn't mean you should spend that much.**

Just because a lender says you qualify for a certain purchase price doesn't mean you should spend that much.

This is a BIG one.

There's a difference between what you technically qualify for and what actually feels comfortable every month.

Your mortgage payment is only one part of your overall financial picture.

Think about property taxes, homeowners insurance, HOA fees when applicable, utilities, maintenance, savings and everything else that comes with owning a home.

Instead of shopping based on the biggest purchase price someone says you can afford, figure out what monthly payment actually fits your lifestyle.

Then work backward from there.

Because being "house poor" is not the homeowner dream.



**4. Budget for More Than Your Mortgage**

Things break.

It's basically a rule of homeownership. 😂

The water heater doesn't care that you just bought the house.

The AC doesn't care that you had a long week.

The fence doesn't care about your budget.

And unfortunately, there's no landlord to call when something goes wrong.

That doesn't mean every homeowner needs to expect a disaster around every corner.

It simply means you should plan for maintenance and unexpected expenses from day one.

Building a homeowner emergency fund can help make those surprise repairs feel a lot less stressful.



**5. Location Is One Thing You Can't Renovate**

You can change the paint.

You can change the flooring.

You can remodel the kitchen.

You can update the bathrooms.

But your street?

Your neighborhood?

Your commute?

The surrounding area?

Those are much harder to change.

When you're buying a home, it's easy to get distracted by beautiful finishes and imagine how amazing the house could look after a few upgrades.

But don't forget to pay attention to everything outside the walls too.

Drive the neighborhood.

Think about your commute.

Check out the surrounding area.

Visit at different times of day if possible.

Think about what your daily life will actually look like there.

The house can change.

The location isn't going anywhere.



**6. Stop Trying to Perfectly "Time" the Market**

There will always be a reason to wait.

Interest rates.

Home prices.

The economy.

Inventory.

Election years.

The news.

There is ALWAYS something happening.

Trying to predict the absolute perfect moment to buy a home can keep you on the sidelines indefinitely.

Instead, focus on whether buying makes sense for YOUR finances, YOUR life and YOUR comfort level with the monthly payment.

The "perfect" market matters a lot less when you're buying a home that you plan to actually live in for years.

Which brings me to the next lesson.



**7. Homeownership Is a Long Game**

There will be years when your home's value increases significantly.

There will be years when the market barely moves.

There may even be periods when values decline.

That's normal.

One thing I wish I had done differently is spend less time obsessing over what my house was supposedly worth every single month.

Zillow says one number.

Another website says something else.

Someone down the street sold for a different price.

And suddenly you're checking your home's estimated value like it's a stock portfolio. 😂

Homeownership is a long game.

Think in YEARS, not weeks or months.

Over time, paying down your mortgage and building equity can become an important part of your overall financial picture.

And honestly, if I could go back and talk to my 30 year old self, I'd probably end with one very simple piece of advice.

**BUY THE HOUSE.**

Not just any house.

Not a house that stretches your budget to the breaking point.

Not a house in a location you don't love.

And definitely not a house just because someone told you that you "should."

Buy the house that makes sense for your life, your finances and your long term goals.

You're going to learn as you go.

You're going to make mistakes.

You're probably going to change things you swore you loved.

That's part of being a homeowner.

But getting into the game and starting to build equity can be a pretty powerful thing.
